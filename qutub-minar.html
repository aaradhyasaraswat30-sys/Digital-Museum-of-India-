Qutub Minar

A magnificent tower that tells the story of India's medieval history.

Introduction

Qutub Minar is a famous historical monument located in Delhi, India. It is one of the tallest brick minarets in the world and is admired for its height, detailed carvings and beautiful Indo-Islamic architecture. It is surrounded by several other important historical structures.

Photograph: Sarah Batra / Wikimedia Commons

History

The construction of Qutub Minar was started by Qutb-ud-din Aibak in 1199. It was later continued and completed by his successor, Iltutmish. Over the years, some parts of the tower were damaged and repaired by different rulers.

The monument represents an important period in India's history and the beginning of the Delhi Sultanate.

Architecture

Qutub Minar is approximately 72.5 metres tall and has five storeys. It is mainly built using red sandstone and marble. Each storey has a balcony, and the tower is decorated with beautiful carvings and inscriptions.

The tower becomes narrower as it rises. Its detailed patterns, calligraphy and unique design make it a remarkable example of Indo-Islamic architecture.

Photograph: Jaanshi / Wikimedia Commons

Interesting Facts

✦ Qutub Minar is approximately 72.5 metres tall.

✦ Its construction began in 1199.

✦ It has five storeys.

✦ It is located in Delhi.

✦ The Qutub Minar complex became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1993.

Legacy

Qutub Minar is an important part of India's cultural heritage. It attracts visitors from around the world and helps us understand the artistic skills, architecture and history of medieval India.
